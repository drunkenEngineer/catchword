# Phase 0 benchmark, 3 October 2026

Backlog items TST-1 and ARC-5. The full tables are in [2026-10-03-tables.md](2026-10-03-tables.md). The decisions are in ADR-20.

## Conditions

- **Machine:** the owner's laptop, Intel i7-13620H (6 performance and 4 efficiency cores), 16 GB of RAM, Windows 11. It is faster than the spec's reference laptop (four cores, about 2020, 8 GB). Speed figures must be measured again on such a machine before release.
- **Evaluation set version 1 (ADR-19):** 72 documents and 2,544 judged queries.
  - XQuAD: 48 Wikipedia articles in English, German and Arabic, with 1,190 descriptive questions, 1,190 cross-language questions and 120 exact names and numbers.
  - The written part: 24 letters, invoices and contracts in English, French and Arabic, with 44 queries.
- **Models:** Granite (the provisional choice) and multilingual-e5-small (the baseline), both in the 8-bit form that would ship.
- **Passage sizes:** 200, 350 and 500 tokens, with overlaps of 30, 50 and 70.
- **Scores:** recall@10, MRR@10 and nDCG@10, as percentages, for words only, meaning only, and both combined.

## 1. Keyword search was fixed first

The first run showed that keyword search, which required every word of the query, almost never matched a question typed as a sentence: recall@10 of 2.1% on descriptive queries. Three changes were measured one at a time, with Granite at 350 tokens.

| Combined search | Every word | Any word | Half the words | Half, common words left out |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| recall@10, all | 91.5 | 69.5 | 92.4 | **92.9** |
| MRR@10, all | 73.0 | 56.3 | 70.9 | **77.8** |
| recall@10 / MRR, descriptive | 94.7 / 77.9 | 99.1 / 89.9 | 98.1 / 88.8 | 97.6 / 88.7 |
| recall@10 / MRR, cross-language | 87.4 / 65.4 | 36.4 / 18.1 | 86.0 / 50.1 | 87.5 / 64.8 |
| recall@10, exact | 98.5 | 98.5 | 98.5 | 98.5 |

What each change did:

- **Any word:** found the questions, but an English question then matched Arabic and German passages through a shared year or name. Rank fusion rewards results found both ways, so these chance matches pushed the right answer down.
- **Half the words:** a passage must contain at least half of the query's distinct words. This removed most chance matches.
- **Common words left out:** words in more than 5% of passages, such as "the", "de" and "in", are left out of the query. This removed the rest of the chance matches, and restored keyword speed (section 3).

The number of results each search contributes before combining was also measured: 10, 20 and 50. The differences were within what a 194-passage corpus can show, so it stays at 50, which also lets the app show more than ten results.

## 2. Models and passage sizes

| Model | Tokens | Passages | Combined recall@10 | Combined nDCG@10 | Meaning, cross-language recall@10 | Arabic, combined recall@10 | Latin script, combined recall@10 | Passages a second | Seconds to embed the set |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Granite | 200 | 313 | 93.5 | 79.4 | 88.8 | 94.3 | 93.1 | 29.8 | 10.5 |
| Granite | 350 | 194 | 92.9 | 80.6 | 87.4 | 94.8 | 92.0 | 19.7 | 9.8 |
| Granite | 500 | 151 | **93.9** | **81.6** | **89.8** | 95.6 | 93.2 | 15.3 | 9.9 |
| e5 | 200 | 323 | 81.8 | 69.9 | 62.0 | 83.7 | 81.0 | 60.5 | 5.3 |
| e5 | 350 | 198 | 79.7 | 68.7 | 57.7 | 80.7 | 79.2 | 39.4 | 5.0 |
| e5 | 500 | 148 | 78.2 | 67.7 | 53.8 | 79.9 | 77.4 | 27.0 | 5.5 |

What the table shows:

- **Quality:** Granite is 11 to 16 points better than e5 on combined recall@10, and 27 to 36 points better on finding passages across languages by meaning. Use case UC9 rests on that.
- **Speed:** e5 embeds about twice as fast. In passages a second, Granite is above the spec's 20 only at 200 tokens. But the time to embed the whole set is the same for Granite at every size, about 10 s, because smaller passages simply means more of them. Size changes how the work is divided, not how much there is.
- **Arabic:** right-to-left documents score within 10 points of Latin-script ones in every configuration. Here they even score higher, which meets the spec's condition for advertising right-to-left support (Q1).
- **Passage size:** the three sizes are within about one point of each other. The small lead of 500 tokens partly reflects the test: a longer passage is more likely to contain a whole answer. Longer passages also mean fewer vectors to search, but a vaguer pointer for the reader.
- **Model-specific details:** e5's 8-bit file is tuned for AVX-512 processors, which this laptop lacks. It may lose some quality here that it would not lose on such a processor. Its 512-token limit was respected at every size.

## 3. Speed at the reference size

These results come from `catchword-eval scale`: 250,000 synthetic passages in 10,000 files, words drawn as in real text (Zipf's law), six-word queries, 384-number vectors. Times are 95th percentiles; the spec's targets are in brackets.

| Measure | First measured | After the write fix | Any word, half must match | Common words left out (now) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Keyword search (under 100 ms) | 31 ms | 30 ms | 305 ms | **68 ms** |
| Vector search | 273 ms | 271 ms | 277 ms | 265 ms |
| Combined search (under 500 ms) | 311 ms | 304 ms | 591 ms | **330 ms** |
| Writing passages and keyword index | 956 s | **55 s** | 55 s | 53 s |
| Index size (about 4 KB a passage) | 2.0 KB | 2.0 KB | 2.0 KB | 2.0 KB |

- **Writing:** writing passages took 16 minutes because each file triggered a scan of the whole index for leftovers, so time grew with the square of the library size. Only the file's own old content is now checked, and the same work takes under a minute.
- **Vector search:** an exact scan of 250,000 vectors now takes most of the combined time. On a slower laptop the combined target may be missed. The spec's next tier is quantised vectors with rescoring (section 14); it should be measured on the reference laptop before 0.1.

## 4. Limits

- **Small corpus.** The corpus is small: 72 documents, about 200 passages. Real libraries hold thousands of documents. Fusion settings and passage size should be measured again on a larger set; XQuAD could be extended with distractor articles.
- **Questions that echo the text.** XQuAD's questions were written by people looking at the paragraph, so they repeat its words. Real queries, from memory, favour meaning search more than this set does.
- **The written part is easy and small.** It scores 100% combined recall, and was written by the same author as the code. It checks coverage of invoice numbers, French, and English-to-French. It does not measure difficulty.
- **No independent reference for the vectors.** Our vectors were not compared with the model's official Python implementation (CORE-5). The scores above are the practical check.
- **Speed was measured on one laptop.** It is faster than the reference machine.
